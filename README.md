# Serialization and the Character-System

Code and data for *Serialization and the Character-System: Measuring Narrative
Concentration in French Fiction (1800–1940)* (CHR 2026).

The paper asks whether the distribution of narrative attention across a novel's
cast has a history. It operationalizes Woloch's *character-system* as the
inequality (Gini coefficient) of the PageRank distribution over a character
network, traces it across 12,011 French novels digitized by Gallica, and tests
what accounts for the trajectory it finds.

**Headline results.** Concentration is U-shaped over the long nineteenth
century, with a trough in the 1850s–1860s. The movement is not the signature of
a genre — detective and adventure fiction are *less* concentrated than the
average novel of their period. What does separate novels is the circuit through
which they reached their readers: across the age of the *roman-feuilleton*
(1840–1914), authors whose careers were structured by newspaper serialization
sustain a markedly more distributed character-system.

## Quick start

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
make test        # 19 unit tests
make figures     # reproduce every figure and result (~10 min)
```

Or work through `notebooks/reproduce.ipynb`, which walks through the whole
argument interactively and runs on the shipped derived tables.

## The derived tables

`data/derived/concentration_*.csv` hold one row per novel:

| column | layer | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `file_name`, `year` | — | `YEAR_Author_Title`, publication year |
| `n_characters`, `n_edges` | — | retained cast (capped at 20) and graph edges |
| `cr1_mentions`, `cr3_mentions`, `gini_mentions`, `hhi_mentions` | attention | from raw mention counts |
| `gini_pagerank`, `cr3_pagerank`, `entropy_pagerank`, `gini_edges` | structure | from weighted PageRank |
| `decoupling` | — | gap between the two layers |
| `is_canon`, `genre`, `is_adventure`, `is_detective` | metadata | see below |

Genre labels are **not** catalogue metadata, which is sparse and unreliable for
popular fiction. They come from supervised retrieval: a classifier trained on a
historically situated seed list, applied to the archive and refined over a
*k*-nearest-neighbour similarity graph. Canon status comes from separate work
aggregating school curricula, prestigious series and literary prizes. Novels
without a label are excluded from the corresponding analyses rather than
treated as an implicit "other" category.

## A note on inference

The serialization label is a property of the **author**, not of the individual
work, so novels by the same writer are not independent observations. Every
estimate involving it is therefore either aggregated to the author level,
fitted with standard errors clustered by author, or tested by permuting labels
across authors — never across novels. `src/charconc/inference.py` implements
all three; treating the 1,887 novels as independent would overstate
significance by roughly five orders of magnitude.

The author lists in `data/labels/authors.yaml` were assigned from publication
history alone, never from the measured concentration, and are grouped so that
`scripts/05` can switch each group on and off. Authors who moved substantially
between the two circuits (Zola, Sand, Daudet, Maupassant, Verne) are excluded
rather than forced into either side.
